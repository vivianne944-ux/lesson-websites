1Trans 1 — Introduction to Basic Pharmacology

A medicine is already approved and marketed. Investigators continue to detect rare and long-term adverse effects in the general population. Which phase of clinical drug investigation is being described?

Answer: D. Phase IV
Phase IV is post-marketing surveillance. It occurs after approval and is useful for detecting uncommon or delayed adverse effects that may not appear in earlier, smaller trials.
Source basis: Trans 1, Clinical Testing / Phases of Clinical Drug Investigation, p. 3
2Trans 1 — Introduction to Basic Pharmacology

Which branch of pharmacology compares the costs of drug therapy with its clinical outcomes or benefits?

Answer: B. Pharmacoeconomics
Pharmacoeconomics evaluates the economic aspects of drug therapy, including the relationship between costs and outcomes. Pharmacognosy studies crude drugs, pharmacogenetics examines inherited variation in drug response, and posology deals with dosage.
Source basis: Trans 1, Branches of Pharmacology, p. 2
3Trans 2 — Nature, Sources & Dosage Forms

A naturally occurring compound is isolated and then chemically modified to improve its therapeutic properties. How is the resulting drug best classified?

Answer: C. Semi-synthetic
A semi-synthetic drug begins with a naturally obtained substance that is chemically modified. A fully synthetic drug is produced through chemical synthesis without using the natural compound as the immediate starting material.
Source basis: Trans 2, Sources of Drugs, p. 3
4Trans 2 — Nature, Sources & Dosage Forms

Which excipient promotes the breakup of a tablet into smaller particles so that dissolution can occur more readily?

Answer: B. Disintegrant
Disintegrants facilitate breakdown of the tablet mass into smaller particles. Binders hold the granulation together, lubricants reduce friction during manufacturing, and preservatives protect against deterioration or contamination.
Source basis: Trans 2, Excipients, p. 2
5Trans 3 — Pharmacokinetics 1

According to Fick’s law, what happens to the rate of passive diffusion if membrane thickness is doubled while all other factors remain unchanged?

Answer: B. It is reduced by half
Diffusion rate is inversely related to membrane thickness. Doubling the diffusion distance therefore reduces the rate to approximately one-half, assuming surface area, concentration gradient, and permeability remain constant.
Source basis: Trans 3, Fick’s Law of Diffusion, p. 2
6Trans 3 — Pharmacokinetics 1

A drug has a very large apparent volume of distribution. Which interpretation is most appropriate?

Answer: C. The drug is extensively distributed into tissues
A large apparent volume of distribution suggests that relatively little drug remains in plasma because much of it has distributed into or bound within tissues.
Source basis: Trans 3, Distribution / Volume of Distribution, p. 4
7Trans 4 — Pharmacokinetics 2

A weakly acidic drug must be eliminated more rapidly through the kidneys. Which manipulation of urine pH would favor its excretion?

Answer: B. Alkalinize the urine
Alkalinizing urine increases ionization of weak acids. Ionized drug is less readily reabsorbed by passive diffusion and is therefore excreted more efficiently.
Source basis: Trans 4, Factors Affecting Drug Excretion, p. 1
8Trans 4 — Pharmacokinetics 2

Which statement best describes first-order elimination kinetics?

Answer: B. A constant fraction is eliminated per unit time
In first-order kinetics, a constant fraction or percentage of the drug is eliminated per unit time. In zero-order kinetics, a constant amount is eliminated per unit time.
Source basis: Trans 4, Clinical Pharmacokinetic Parameters, p. 4
9Trans 5 — Pharmacodynamics & Drug Receptors

A tissue reaches maximal response even though not all receptors are occupied. Which concept best explains this finding?

Answer: B. Spare receptors
Spare receptors are present when maximal effect can be produced without occupancy of all receptors. The lecture notes this relationship when Kd is greater than EC50.
Source basis: Trans 5, Drug–Receptor Coupling / Spare Receptors, p. 1
10Trans 5 — Pharmacodynamics & Drug Receptors

Which dose–response curve is most useful for determining the median effective dose, median toxic dose, and variability of response in a population?

Answer: B. Quantal dose–response curve
A quantal dose–response curve describes the proportion of a population showing a defined response and is used to estimate ED50, TD50, therapeutic index, and population variability.
Source basis: Trans 5, Quantal Dose–Response Curve, p. 3
11Trans 6 — Rational Drug Therapy

What is the first step in the process of rational drug prescribing?

Answer: B. Define the patient’s problem
Rational prescribing begins by evaluating and defining the patient’s problem or diagnosis. Therapeutic objectives and treatment selection follow afterward.
Source basis: Trans 6, Ethical Prescribing / P-drug Process, pp. 1–2
12Trans 6 — Rational Drug Therapy

Which consideration belongs primarily under the suitability component of the ESSC criteria?

Answer: B. Frequency and convenience of dosing
Suitability includes patient-specific contraindications, comorbidities, interactions, dosage form, dose frequency, convenience, and availability. Mechanism and evidence support efficacy, while total treatment expense belongs to cost.
Source basis: Trans 6, P-drug Selection: ESSC Criteria, pp. 2–3
13Trans 7 — Adverse Drug Reactions

A patient abruptly stops long-term clonidine therapy and develops rebound hypertension. Which type of adverse drug reaction is this?

Answer: D. Type E — End of use
Type E reactions occur after cessation of a drug and manifest as withdrawal or rebound effects. Clonidine withdrawal may cause rebound hypertension.
Source basis: Trans 7, Type E (End of Use), pp. 4–5
14Trans 7 — Adverse Drug Reactions

An antibiotic is taken correctly, but the infection persists because the organism is resistant. Which ADR category best fits this event?

Answer: D. Type F — Failure of efficacy
Type F is unexpected therapeutic failure. Causes listed in the lecture include microbial resistance, poor adherence, interactions, incorrect administration, poor absorption, and disease progression.
Source basis: Trans 7, Type F (Failure of Efficacy), p. 5
15Trans 8 — NSAIDs, Paracetamol, DMARDs & Gout

Ketorolac is selected for postoperative pain. What is the recommended maximum duration of treatment emphasized in the lecture?

Answer: C. 5 days
Ketorolac is used for short-term treatment of moderate to severe or postoperative pain and should not be used for more than 5 days because toxicity, particularly renal toxicity, increases with continued use.
Source basis: Trans 8, Ketorolac, pp. 5–6
16Trans 8 — NSAIDs, Paracetamol, DMARDs & Gout

A patient presents with an acute gout flare. Which drug is used to relieve the current inflammatory attack rather than chronically lower serum urate?

Answer: C. Colchicine
Colchicine is used for acute gout and inhibits leukocyte migration and phagocytosis. Allopurinol and febuxostat are xanthine oxidase inhibitors for chronic urate-lowering therapy, while pegloticase is reserved for chronic refractory gout.
Source basis: Trans 8, Drugs Used in Acute and Chronic Gout, pp. 7–9
17Trans 9 — Opioid Analgesics

Which pair of neuronal effects is produced by opioid receptor activation?

Answer: B. Close presynaptic Ca²⁺ channels and open postsynaptic K⁺ channels
Opioid Gi/Go-coupled receptors close voltage-gated calcium channels presynaptically, reducing transmitter release, and open potassium channels postsynaptically, causing hyperpolarization.
Source basis: Trans 9, Pharmacodynamics / Mechanism of Action, p. 3
18Trans 9 — Opioid Analgesics

A patient is comatose with pinpoint pupils and respiratory depression after suspected opioid exposure. Which immediate pharmacologic treatment is indicated after support of vital functions?

Answer: C. Naloxone
Pinpoint pupils, respiratory depression, and coma form the classic opioid toxicity triad. Naloxone is the opioid antagonist used for reversal, together with airway and ventilatory support.
Source basis: Trans 9, Opioid Toxicity and Naloxone, p. 4
19Laboratory — Prescription Writing (Solids)

Which part of a prescription contains directions to the pharmacist regarding the quantity or preparation to dispense?

Answer: C. Subscription
The subscription contains directions to the pharmacist. The signatura contains directions to the patient, the inscription identifies the drug and strength, and the superscription is the Rx symbol.
Source basis: Prescription Writing (Solids), Parts of a Prescription, p. 1
20Laboratory — Prescription Writing (Solids)

Which entry follows the prescription-writing conventions in the laboratory handout?

Answer: C. 0.5 mL
Use a leading zero before decimals, write milliliters rather than cubic centimeters, and use Arabic rather than Roman numerals. Therefore, “0.5 mL” is the correct form.
Source basis: Prescription Writing (Solids), Metric and Formatting Rules, pp. 1–2
21Laboratory — Prescription Writing (Solids)

Ibuprofen 400 mg tablets are ordered as 1 tablet every 8 hours round the clock for 5 days. How many tablets should be dispensed?

Answer: B. 15 tablets
Every 8 hours round the clock equals 3 doses per 24 hours. One tablet × 3 doses/day × 5 days = 15 tablets.
Source basis: Prescription Writing (Solids), Quantity Computation, pp. 1–2
22Laboratory — Prescription Writing (Liquids)

An 18-kg child is prescribed paracetamol 10 mg/kg/dose every 6 hours round the clock for 2 days. The preparation is 120 mg/5 mL. What total volume is required?

Answer: C. 60 mL
Dose = 18 kg × 10 mg/kg = 180 mg. At 120 mg/5 mL, 180 mg equals 7.5 mL. Every 6 hours gives 4 doses/day; 7.5 mL × 4 × 2 days = 60 mL.
Source basis: Prescription Writing (Liquids), Weight-based Dose and Total-volume Computation, pp. 1–2
