Big pattern

Only three 2028 items are completely new in this mapping. Most of the paper either directly repeats a prior item or reuses the same concept in a new scenario. The strongest recurrence zones are confidentiality, informed consent, and the principles of double effect, cooperation, stewardship, and totality.

How to use this for review

Start with direct repeats, then learn the rule behind the concept clusters. The exam often changes the surface story while preserving the same ethical test.

PriorityWhat to masterWhy it matters
1 · MemorizeMedical vs. bioethics; non-maleficence; Nuremberg legacy; Roe; Quinlan; ethics committee; confidentiality breach exceptions; verbal consent.These contain near-verbatim repeats or repeated answer structures.
2 · Drill casesDuty to warn/Tarasoff, child-abuse reporting, emergency consent, morphine at end of life, gangrenous-limb amputation, cooperation under duress.The scenario changes, but the governing rule remains stable.
3 · Build a rule sheetFour conditions of double effect; autonomy and confidentiality; capacity/surrogates; stewardship; formal vs. material cooperation; totality.These concepts generate multiple differently worded questions.
4 · Patch gapsThomas Percival, therapeutic privilege, express consent, Tuskegee details, PMA confidentiality after death, research disclosure, ethical theories, and justice subtypes.They are less recurrent in these files but remain plausible one-off test items.
